Project Overview

Managing water resources and agricultural planning across Sri Lanka's diverse climatic zones requires long-term, high-resolution climate analytics rather than static seasonal approximations.

Engineered on an enterprise distributed big-data architecture, this system analyzes regional precipitation extremes and shifting seasonal trends while modeling low-evapotranspiration events during critical agricultural transition windows.

Business & Environmental Impact: Replaces static water-allocation schedules with dynamic, weather-driven evapotranspiration estimates—mitigating agricultural risks during the Maha and Yala seasons and optimizing reservoir usage.

1. Multi-Tier Distributed Processing Pipeline

The processing architecture splits workload responsibilities across three dedicated engine layers to balance batch throughput and in-memory execution speed:

  • Batch Storage & Computation (Hadoop MapReduce): Processed raw daily weather records across all Sri Lankan districts to compute monthly precipitation/temperature aggregates and isolate historical rainfall extremes.
  • Structured Analytics (Apache Hive): Formulated distributed SQL queries to compute seasonal evapotranspiration averages across primary agricultural seasons (Maha and Yala) and identify temperate urban microclimates.
  • In-Memory Analytics (Apache Spark / PySpark): Executed high-throughput solar radiation threshold analysis and calculated weekly temperature extremes during peak heat months.

2. Predictive Modeling with Spark MLlib

To assist agricultural planners during May—a critical planting and irrigation transition window—a distributed regression pipeline was constructed in Spark MLlib to model factors driving low evapotranspiration:

Feature Pipeline: Solar radiation, precipitation hours, sunshine duration, average temperature, and wind speed assembled via VectorAssembler and scaled prior to model training.

Workflow: Vectorization, feature scaling, 80/20 train-test splitting, and regression model evaluation performed entirely in-memory.

Primary Insight: Extended precipitation hours combined with decreased sunshine duration served as the primary co-drivers of low evapotranspiration, providing actionable parameters for dynamic irrigation scheduling.

Key Insights & Technical Trade-offs

  • 01
    In-Memory vs. Disk-Based Execution: Migrating complex time-series threshold calculations from MapReduce to Spark in-memory DataFrames reduced iterative analysis runtime significantly across the multi-year dataset.
  • 02
    Feature Engineering for Evapotranspiration: Combining sunshine duration and precipitation hours as interaction features in Spark MLlib yielded higher predictive accuracy for low ET days than using temperature alone.
  • 03
    Query-Level Data Quality Enforcement: Hive queries validated date formats via regex and excluded null evapotranspiration records before aggregation, with season classification (Maha/Yala) derived directly from parsed date components rather than a pre-built column.
