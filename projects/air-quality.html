Project Overview

An unsupervised machine learning and statistical drift analysis framework applied to a full year of hourly multisensor air quality data (9,358 readings) from an Italian city.

By fitting independent K-Means clustering models across distinct seasonal periods (warm vs. cold), this project quantifies how underlying urban pollution regimes drift over time—evaluating centroid shifts, cluster membership redistributions, and Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence to demonstrate why static models fail in dynamic environmental monitoring systems.

Business & Environmental Impact: Demonstrates the failure modes of static monitoring models under seasonal environmental shifts, and proposes ongoing KL divergence monitoring as a practical signal for when re-clustering may be warranted.

Drift Analysis

Figure 1: End-to-end seasonal clustering, alignment, and statistical drift pipeline.

1. Data Engineering & Feature Preparation

To process raw sensor telemetry while preserving genuine pollution spikes, a robust data engineering workflow was established:

  • Null Handling & Feature Removal: Removed features with severe missingness ($>90\%$ missing, e.g., Non-Metanic Hydrocarbons / NMHC). Mapped sentinel error codes (-200) to nulls and imputed remaining missing values via iterative multivariate and linear interpolation.
  • Signal Preservation: Outlier readings reflecting legitimate pollution spikes (e.g., peak traffic periods) were bounded using Winsorization rather than deletion.
  • Distribution Normalization: Applied log-transformations followed by RobustScaler to minimize scaling distortion caused by right-skewed pollutant concentrations.

2. Seasonal Clustering & Drift Measurement

K-Means models ($k=3$) were trained independently on Period 1 (Warm: Mar–Aug 2004) and Period 2 (Cold: Sep 2004–Mar 2005). Clusters converged on three distinct operational states: Low, Moderate, and High pollution.

Before quantifying drift across runs, Period 2 clusters were aligned to Period 1 counterparts using minimum Euclidean centroid distance matching.

Centroid Shifts: High-Pollution cluster shifted significantly due to elevated $\text{NO}_x$ and $\text{NO}_2$ concentrations in winter.

Membership Redistribution: Moderate cluster shrank by 27.1%, while Low (+23.6%) and High (+21.5%) clusters expanded dramatically.

Kullback–Leibler (KL) Divergence: Calculated at $D_{\text{KL}} = 0.0329$, indicating localized, cluster-specific drift rather than total structural breakdown.

Key Insights & MLOps Takeaways

  • 01
    Concept & Data Drift in Environmental Systems: Environmental models suffer from severe seasonal drift. Atmospheric boundary layer changes in winter trap primary pollutants closer to ground level, altering feature correlations.
  • 02
    Failure of Static Models: A single static clustering model trained on warm-season data under-predicts the frequency and severity of extreme winter pollution events.
  • 03
    Continuous Monitoring Strategy: Recommends tracking KL divergence between successive periods as an early-warning signal, with a validated threshold to be established through further testing before triggering automated re-clustering.
