Project Overview

A scalable, cloud-native machine learning framework built on AWS SageMaker to forecast half-hourly regional electricity demand across five Australian states (New South Wales, Victoria, Queensland, South Australia, and Tasmania).

By integrating two decades of high-frequency weather and grid load data, the system addresses the regional divergence in energy consumption—training localized ensemble models, optimizing hyperparameter spaces per state, and deploying a stacked meta-model to ensure grid stability and operational efficiency.

Strategic Grid Impact: Replaces uniform macro models with localized state-level ensembles to address regional climate divergence, enhancing operational efficiency and grid stability across eastern Australia.

1. Data Engineering & Feature Pipeline

Data was processed on AWS SageMaker, combining half-hourly weather parameters (temperature, humidity, pressure, precipitation) with regional grid load records:

  • Temporal Alignment: Executed time-aware merge_asof joins to synchronize asynchronous weather sensor logs with electricity demand time-series, resampling the unified output into an hourly modeling table.
  • Feature Engineering: Derived domain-specific features, including Wet Bulb Temperature (capturing joint heat-humidity stress), temperature–dew point spreads, and station-to-sea level pressure variances.
  • Calendar & Behavioral Indicators: Engineered cyclic features capturing time-of-day, day-of-week, quarter, weekend indicators, and public holidays.
  • Outlier Mitigation: Applied per-region Interquartile Range (IQR) bounds and Winsorization to manage sensor anomalies without discarding extreme weather events.

2. Exploratory Data Insights & Regional Variance

Data analysis revealed distinct operational dynamics across regions, demonstrating that a single national model would fail to capture localized grid drivers:

  • Climate Driver Contrast: Queensland (QLD) demand peaked during summer due to heavy cooling loads (strong positive correlation with temperature), whereas New South Wales (NSW), Victoria (VIC), and Tasmania (TAS) experienced winter peaks driven by heating demand.
  • Top Weather Signal: Wet Bulb Temperature emerged as the strongest weather-related predictor ($r = 0.394$), outperforming raw dry-bulb air temperature across all states.
  • Load Profiles: Identified consistent daily double-peak structures across all regions—a startup peak between 07:00–09:00 and a primary peak between 18:00–21:00.

Key Insights & Feature Importance Analysis

Across all five state models, feature attribution analysis highlighted the relative impact of temporal and atmospheric inputs:

  • 01
    Hour of Day & Day of Year: Primary drivers of baseline grid load shapes, accounting for underlying daily schedule routines and macroscopic annual cycles.
  • 02
    Wet Bulb Temperature ($r = 0.394$): Emerged as the single strongest weather predictor across all five state models, effectively capturing the compound human comfort index that triggers HVAC loads.
  • 03
    Temperature-Dew Point Spread: Key contributor during transitional seasonal months, providing early variance signals during rapid atmospheric pressure shifts.
