Project Overview

Automated screening of Diabetic Retinopathy (DR) demands high accuracy alongside verifiable clinical safety, calibration, and resilience against out-of-distribution real-world image artifacts.

This project evaluates two distinct deep learning architectures across five clinical severity stages (Grade 0: Healthy to Grade 4: Proliferative), benchmarking performance on Quadratic Weighted Kappa (QWK), model calibration, Grad-CAM interpretability, and prevalence bias across 53,000 unlabeled real-world images.

Clinical Impact: Establishes a trustworthy automated triage system capable of catching life-altering Proliferative cases (Grade 4) while delivering calibrated risk scores to support ophthalmologist decision-making.

1. Data Engineering & Preprocessing Pipeline

To eliminate patient-level data leakage (where 87% of patients had matching disease grades in both eyes) and standardize varied fundus imaging conditions, raw images underwent a strict five-stage preprocessing workflow:

  • Patient-Level Data Splitting: Enforced zero cross-eye leakage between training and testing splits.
  • Automated Border Cropping & Radius Scaling: Removed non-informative black borders and normalized all retinas to a uniform spatial scale.
  • Square Padding & Circular Masking: Prevented aspect ratio distortion while eliminating camera border artifacts.
  • Ben Graham Contrast Enhancement: Isolates channel color variances to amplify subtle microaneurysms and exudates.
  • Imbalance Mitigation: Countered severe class imbalance (73.5% Grade 0 vs. <5% Grades 3–4) via focal loss, weighted sampling, and Mixup data augmentation.

2. Model Architecture & Training Strategy

Two distinct architectural philosophies were evaluated under identical patient splits:

Transfer Learning (EfficientNet-B4): Pre-trained on ImageNet and fine-tuned using a two-phase strategy (frozen warm-up followed by progressive unfreezing with discriminative learning rates).

Custom Architecture (RetinalNet): Built from scratch featuring a ResNet-style stem, four residual stages integrated with Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) attention modules, Kaiming initialization, and a warmup schedule.

Performance & Clinical Metrics

Because DR grades are ordinal (misgrading Grade 0 as Grade 4 is far worse than misgrading as Grade 1), models were evaluated using Quadratic Weighted Kappa (QWK) alongside Proliferative DR (Grade 4) sensitivity.

Key Insights & Clinical Failure Analysis

  • 01
    Grad-CAM Visual Audit: Gradient heatmaps confirmed that both models focused on clinically relevant features (microaneurysms, hemorrhages, and exudates) rather than camera border artifacts or lighting gradients.
  • 02
    Calibration vs. Recall Trade-off: EfficientNet-B4 achieved higher Grade 4 sensitivity (74%), making it ideal for safety-first triage. However, RetinalNet demonstrated superior probability calibration, producing true risk probabilities suitable for medical decision support.
  • 03
    Large-Scale Bias Audit (53,000 Images): Evaluating both networks on 53,000 unlabeled fundus images revealed an over-prediction skew toward "Healthy" (Grade 0) caused by real-world class distributions, with RetinalNet exhibiting less bias drift.

Deployment Strategy & Recommendations

1. Automated Screening & Triage

Deploy EfficientNet-B4 in primary screening environments where maximizing Grade 4 Proliferative recall (74%) is critical to preventing unmonitored vision loss.

2. Decision Support & Risk Stratification

Deploy RetinalNet in clinical decision support settings requiring well-calibrated confidence scores and precision across intermediate disease stages.

3. Dual-Model Hybrid Ensemble

Implement a production ensemble that combines EfficientNet’s high-grade sensitivity with RetinalNet’s calibrated confidence probabilities for balanced clinical decision-making.
